Wikipedia is a free, multilingual online encyclopedia created and maintained by a global community of volunteer editors. Launched in January 2001 by Jimmy Wales and Larry Sanger, it operates under an open-collaboration model that allows anyone with internet access to write and alter its articles.

The platform is hosted and funded by the Wikimedia Foundation, a non-profit organization that relies primarily on user donations rather than advertisements. Over more than two decades, it has grown into one of the largest and most widely used reference websites in human history.

With content available in over 300 languages, Wikipedia plays a massive role in global digital inclusion and open-access education. It serves as a vital first stop for researchers, students, and general knowledge seekers looking for quick overviews on nearly any topic imaginable.

To maintain factual integrity, the community enforces strict editorial policies requiring articles to be written from a neutral point of view. Contributors must cite reliable, published sources to verify all claims and to prevent the inclusion of original research.

While its open nature makes it vulnerable to occasional vandalism or misinformation, a massive network of automated bots and human administrators continuously monitors edits. This collective oversight ensures that errors are usually corrected within minutes, preserving the site's overall reliability.
